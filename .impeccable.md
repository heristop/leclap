## Design Context

### Users

Developers and teams who need reproducible, reviewable video from software workflows — including AI coding agents that attach proof to pull requests — and creators who shoot and render on their phone. They arrive curious and skeptical: they want to see the real thing working (a real render, a real capture, a real PR) before they read a claim. The job: turn a template plus their media into a finished video, anywhere — terminal, browser, phone, agent toolchain.

### Brand Personality

Bold, juicy, precise. Cinematic but honest: every claim is backed by a real render ("composition, not generation"). The voice is direct and technical, with dry, understated humor in public copy (never invented anecdotes). The mascot, Clappy — the logo's clapperboard redrawn by hand and gently chubby — adds warmth and appears sparingly. Emotional goals: "wow, it runs on my phone", confidence, a smile.

### Aesthetic Direction

- Near-black ink canvas, the lavender→pink brand gradient (#7C83FD → #FF8AAE) reserved for the moments that matter (primary CTA, one gradient word, playheads), pastel yellow (#FFF685) as a rare accent. Tokens in `apps/leclap-web/src/index.css`; brand rules in `DESIGN.md`.
- Oswald, condensed and uppercase for display; oversized kinetic headlines; generous negative space.
- Reference: Apple product pages — one idea per viewport, cinematic full-bleed film, sticky scroll-driven storytelling where a device stays pinned while its screen changes, restrained motion that follows the scroll, calm confident copy.
- The product proves itself: real screen captures, real renders, real device footage. No stock mockups, no fake UI.
- Anti-references: generic SaaS template pages (gradient blobs, feature-card grids as the main story), AI-video-generator hype aesthetics, busy neon cyberpunk, empty or placeholder states on screen.
- Dark-first (the landing sections force `.dark`); light theme exists app-wide.

### Design Principles

1. Show the real thing. Every section is anchored on a real capture, render or film — never a placeholder or an empty state.
2. One idea per screen. Each viewport makes one point, with one gradient accent; scroll advances the story.
3. Motion with meaning. Scroll-linked and eased (the shared `--ease-*` curves); text fades up whole; everything settles under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
4. Precise claims. Deterministic per platform, on-device where possible, LeClap renders and the workflow attaches — say exactly that.
5. Localized and accessible by default. Copy in en/fr/de/es/it with no hardcoded strings; keyboard, visible focus, captions on films, readable contrast.
